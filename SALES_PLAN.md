# CertDesk launch and sales plan

## Positioning

> Every tenant and contractor document, renewal, and missing certificate—without the spreadsheet chase.

CertDesk is for owner-operators and small teams managing roughly 20–200 units and 50–200 active vendors or contractors. It records documents and expiration dates; it does not provide legal or insurance sufficiency advice.

## Founding offer

- Starter: $29/month, up to 75 tracked documents
- Growth: $49/month, up to 200 tracked documents
- Founding customers keep the price for 12 months
- Paid pilot: $99 for the first 30 days, applied to the first subscription

## Validate before adding features

1. Interview 15 property managers managing 20–200 units.
2. Ask for a redacted list of 10 document requests and one spreadsheet.
3. Manually reproduce the workflow in a demo workspace.
4. Offer a $99 paid pilot to the first five qualified teams.
5. Build further only after three teams pay or sign a paid-pilot agreement.

Interview questions:

- How do you currently collect certificates from vendors?
- How many hours per week do you spend chasing or checking them?
- What happens when a document expires?
- Who needs access, and what would stop you from paying for a tool?
- Which export or email workflow would make renewal tracking reliable?

## First acquisition channels

1. **Property-management associations:** ask for a 10-minute workflow session; do not start with a generic pitch.
2. **Cold email to 50–500-unit managers:** mention one workflow and include a 20-second screen recording.
3. **Insurance brokers:** a broker who repeatedly chases certificates can recommend the tracker.
4. **Local property-manager groups:** offer a short spreadsheet-cleanup clinic.
5. **Founder referrals:** give each paid customer two additional invites only after they reach value.

Example outreach:

> Hi {{ name }} — property managers often lose time chasing vendor certificates across email and spreadsheets. I built a small tracker that collects documents through a one-use link and flags expirations. I mapped your workflow for {{ company }} in a 3-minute demo. Would a 15-minute pilot be useful?

Do not claim legal compliance, replacement for a full compliance platform, or guaranteed savings.

## Sales assets

- Product tour: record the landing page, demo workspace, request creation, vendor upload, and renewal dashboard.
- One-page PDF: problem, workflow, limits, price, and founder offer.
- Demo URL: use the seeded demo with synthetic documents only.
- Security sheet: hosting, file access, backup, retention, and deletion policy.
- Onboarding checklist: add properties, vendors, rules, send first request, and import a spreadsheet.

## Revenue model

The product is a low-maintenance recurring SaaS, not truly passive income. Hosting, support, sales, email delivery, and compliance updates require ongoing work. A realistic goal is 10 paying customers at $39 average monthly revenue = about $390 MRR, before fees and taxes.

## After the first five customers

Prioritize in this order:

1. Reliability and backup restoration
2. Recurring request templates
3. Bulk vendor onboarding
4. Reminder cadence preferences
5. CSV/spreadsheet import
6. Optional Growth-plan AI extraction, staff-triggered with human review
7. Stripe cancellation and failed-payment handling
8. Customer portal and invitations

Do not build native apps, a public API, complex coverage-rule engines, or multi-property accounting integrations before repeat demand is demonstrated.
